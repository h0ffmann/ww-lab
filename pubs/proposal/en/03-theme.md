# THEME

Third-generation spectral wave models solve the wave action balance equation on a spatial grid and a spectral grid of frequencies and directions, integrating the physics of wind input, nonlinear interactions and dissipation at every point and every time step. WAVEWATCH III® (WW3), developed at NOAA/NCEP, is the reference model of this class and the one adopted by most operational centres, research groups and industry users worldwide [@ww3manual; @ww3repo]. It is written in Fortran, parallelised with MPI and OpenMP, and its behaviour is fixed at compile time by a *switch file* that selects the physics packages, the propagation scheme and the I/O options.

The Laboratório de Engenharia e Ciências Oceânicas (LabECO) of the Department of Mechanical Engineering at the Universidade Federal de Santa Catarina (UFSC) runs WW3 as part of its contribution to the Rede Nacional de Observação e Monitoramento Oceânico (ReNOMO), a national network funded by the CNPq/MCTI/Finep 062/2022 call that integrates ocean observation, quality control and operational product delivery for the South Atlantic and the Brazilian coast [@renomo; @labeco]. In an operational setting the cost of a forecast is measured in wall-clock time: the time between the arrival of the atmospheric forcing and the delivery of wave products bounds how often the model can be run, at which resolution, on how many nested domains, and how many ensemble members or sensitivity runs fit into a day of computing.

The theme of this project is therefore the systematic reduction of the wall-clock time of WW3 in the configuration used by LabECO, treated as an engineering problem with measurable outcomes: a reproducible benchmark of the current run, a profile that says where the time goes, and a ladder of optimisations of increasing depth, from build options that cost nothing to change, through the run configuration, to targeted refactoring of the hottest routines in modern Fortran, and finally to a feasibility study of executing the model on a data-centre GPU (NVIDIA H100). Each rung is measured on the laboratory's own configuration and accepted only if the results remain within a tolerance agreed with the scientists, so that the operational chain never trades correctness for speed.
